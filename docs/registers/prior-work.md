# Prior Acoustic Manipulation Work — Bounded Comparison

**Prepared:** 2026-10-03 · **Work item:** LIT-05 · **Search status:** two scoped primary-source search cycles plus focused closed-loop-control, acoustic-gravitation terminology, beat-driven-dynamics and prescribed-trajectory follow-ups; not an exhaustive systematic review

## Search scope and interpretation

The searches targeted acoustic levitation/positioning, phased-array manipulation, force measurement, reduced-gravity droplet motion, microgravity sample handling, recent microgravity acoustic trapping, closed-loop trajectory following and acoustic “gravitation-like” interpretations. Search cycles used combinations of `acoustic levitation microgravity force acceleration large object`, `phased array 3D particle manipulation`, `reduced gravity droplet acoustic manipulation`, `microgravity acoustic positioning`, `acoustic array feedback trajectory control`, `acoustic radiation force gravitation-like field` and cited primary-source follow-up. Primary papers, preprints and NASA technical reports were checked for bibliographic identity and the specific reported experiment or measured quantity. This is a bounded search, not a novelty clearance, systematic review or claim that unlocated work does not exist.

## What a distinct AURA question could test

The literature already includes force characterization, levitation, 3D manipulation, feedback trajectory following, experimentally tested cosine-acceleration motion, model-based feedforward path following, reduced-gravity droplet handling, microgravity biological trapping, beat-driven trajectories and a theoretical “gravitation-like” interpretation for a Rayleigh sphere. Abdelaziz and Grier's specific beating-wave mechanism is predicted to disappear in microgravity because it relies on gravity-induced displacement; that prediction does not establish what other acoustic mechanisms can do there. The potentially distinguishing *question* therefore cannot be generic prescribed-motion generation: for an explicitly frozen body, medium, field, workspace and time interval, can a traceable field-to-force-to-dynamics model predict and maintain a prescribed center-of-mass acceleration in AURA's selected campaign domain within a predeclared uncertainty criterion, and how does that prediction fail as scale or geometry changes? This is a candidate research question only. It is not a novelty finding: the search is not exhaustive, the target is not selected, and no AURA force, dynamics, robustness or measurement result exists.

Net center-of-mass acceleration alone would establish only a body- and condition-specific force result. Any later use of “pseudogravity” must separately define whether it means that operational result or a stronger gravity-like property such as response across multiple test bodies, spatial extent, persistence and perturbation tolerance. A controller's ability to compensate a nonuniform field must not be conflated with the uncontrolled field quality; those are separate observables for a later frozen protocol.

## Search gaps and follow-up

Before a novelty claim or target selection, repeat the search for the exact selected geometry/material and target observable, including closely related force-control, acceleration-tracking, acoustic-radiation-force and reduced-gravity literature. Inspect citations and supplementary measurement data; distinguish levitation equilibrium, trap translation, sample localization, net force, prescribed acceleration and gravity-equivalence tests. Search completeness and the state of the literature must be refreshed at the claim/release gate. No author outreach, paid access or acquisition was performed.

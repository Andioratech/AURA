# NUM-03 Intermediate Review — Special-Function Primitives

**Date:** 2026-10-03 · **Disposition:** Intermediate software checks PASS; NUM-03 remains ACTIVE

## Reviewed scope

This review covers isolated binary64 spherical Bessel/Legendre and Gauss-Legendre routines, the stationary sound-hard sphere coefficient and Hasegawa source factors in `src/aura/fields/numerical.py`, and the separate truncated plane-wave/rigid-sphere reference in `src/aura/fields/_plane_sphere_reference.py`. The reference is a verification fixture using an independent source geometry; it reuses the checked special-function kernels. It is not the Hasegawa field solver, and no piston/sphere coupled air field was evaluated.

## Evidence

- 217 focused NUM-03 source/kernel tests pass in ENV-1.0 after correcting the source diffraction integral against Hasegawa et al.'s Eq. 2 and adding high-order and Rayleigh-source checks.
- Spherical `j_n` is compared with a separately evaluated 160-digit Decimal power series for orders 0–12 at four positive arguments, including one above the oscillatory-region start requirement.
- A far-exterior probe found loss of relative accuracy in Miller-normalized `j_n` at large argument and low order (argument about 446.7); errors reached about `2e-8` for `n=0` and `1.9e-3` for `n=1` near argument 2279. In the oscillatory regime, the implementation now uses upward recurrence for `j_n` while retaining Miller recurrence near and above the turning region. Five selected orders (0, 1, 8, 16 and 32) at argument `446.664123` match independently summed 340-digit Decimal values and derivatives within `2e-12` relative tolerance; an additional `n=1` value/derivative check at argument 2279 uses 1,400-digit Decimal series and the same tolerance. The supported argument remains bounded at 8192; these selected-point checks do not establish accuracy over the full argument/order domain or resolve large-order Neumann overflow.
- Recurrence/derivative identities are checked for `j_n` and `y_n`; Legendre parity and derivative finite differences are checked independently.
- Gauss-Legendre nodes integrate polynomial moments through degree `2N-1` for orders 1–9; the stationary sphere coefficient enforces its modal zero-normal-velocity condition for orders 0–15 at `ka=11.45` and matches a separate 160-digit Decimal coefficient reference for orders 0–16 at the frozen air `ka` (including stationary `n=1`). This is coefficient-level reference evidence, not yet a sampled exact plane-wave scattering comparison.
- The `n=0` and `n=1` piston source factors are checked against independent composite Simpson quadrature of Eq. 2 and the primary paper's closed forms in Eq. 4; an undersized local workspace is rejected before quadrature starts.
- Four axial source-normalization cases reconstruct Hasegawa Eq. 1 in the project's `exp(-iwt)` convention and compare pressure with the exact axial integral of the baffled-disk Rayleigh surface kernel, `Phi = V0/(i k) [exp(i k sqrt(z^2+R^2)) - exp(i k z)]`; the Rayleigh surface-integral formulation is given by Cheng, Lin and Qin (2011), [doi:10.1016/j.ultras.2010.12.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultras.2010.12.011). For the frozen air geometry at the near gap, the relative complex-potential residual fell from `1.93e-2` at order 16 to `9.75e-12` at order 240 on the piston-facing sphere pole, and from `4.21e-2` to `1.13e-11` on the rear pole. At the far gap, it reached `2.5e-15` by order 48 on the front pole and `3.0e-15` on the rear pole. These scalar checks do not set the production truncation criterion.
- The first order-96 near-gap source-factor attempt failed because squaring the unscaled Miller recurrence values overflowed. Normalizing the recurrence pair by its maximum magnitude before forming the projection fixed the overflow; order-96 `j_n(11.5)` now agrees with an independent 160-digit Decimal series. This is a numerical-kernel correction, not evidence of field convergence.
- A separate prototype returns the irregular spherical solution `y_n` as a binary64 mantissa plus a base-2 exponent, rescaling the recurrence `y_(n+1) = ((2n+1)/x)y_n - y_(n-1)` from [NIST DLMF 10.51.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.51.E1) before values exceed the floating-point range. At `x=11.45`, the represented order-256 value lies above binary64's exponent range and matches an independently evaluated 160-digit Decimal recurrence within `3e-14` relative error. This validates only the isolated representation; the Hasegawa source integral and stationary-sphere product do not yet consume it, and their order-256 failure remains open.
- An exploratory source/scattering-factor balance used the frozen DEC-002 air geometry (`f=25.23 kHz`, `c=346 m/s`, sphere radius `25 mm`, gap `0.1 mm`, piston radius `10 mm`) with quadrature order 64 and an `80,000,000`-byte local cap. `f_n` was evaluated in binary64 through order 160; the stationary-sphere coefficient magnitude was independently evaluated from 380-digit Decimal `j_n/y_n` recurrences. Across sampled orders 96–160, the largest `log10|c_n f_n|` was `-82.8893` at order 96; at order 160 it was `-174.0464`, while the binary64 coefficient itself underflowed to zero. The sampled sequence was not strictly monotone, and this product omits the outgoing radial factor, angular weighting, and derivative weights. It is neither a truncation bound nor a full modal field term; it only shows why the need for scaled multiplication must be decided from a complete observable-specific tail analysis.
- An exploratory axial-derivative series diagnostic used the same near-gap frozen air geometry at the front and rear sphere poles. The relative residual against the analytic Rayleigh-disk axial derivative changed from `3.1770e-10` (front) and `2.3870e-10` (rear) at order 240 to `1.8936e-10` and `8.5361e-11` at the maximum currently representable source order 253; intermediate orders 245 and 250 were not monotone at both poles. Raising source-factor quadrature from 64 to 128 changed those order-253 residuals by about `1e-13`, much less than the remaining discrepancy. The exploratory `2e-11` target is still missed; this evidence does not justify relaxing it or selecting production truncation. Order 256 still fails closed in the unscaled source-factor routine, so a scaled weighted-series path and an observable-specific remainder analysis remain open.
- Six off-axis source-field cases compare pressure, both axisymmetric velocity components and their pressure gradients at sphere-surface points with polar angles 60, 90 and 120 degrees at both gap endpoints. The independent disk oracle uses composite Simpson radial integration and periodic trapezoidal azimuthal integration, each at 512 intervals. Refining both dimensions from 256 to 512 changed the near-gap Rayleigh potential and gradient components by at most `1.91e-9` relative and the far-gap values by at most `8.57e-11`. At the 120-degree point, a further refinement from 512 to 1024 changed the near-gap values by at most `1.20e-10` and the far-gap values by `5.35e-12`. The fixed comparison check uses `5e-9` relative tolerance to cover reference quadrature error and binary64 summation; it is a bounded component regression, not a production field truncation criterion. Broader maps and the far-field limit remain open.
- An exploratory attempt to check the axial source-series derivative against the analytic Rayleigh derivative with `2e-11` relative tolerance failed at the minimum gap. At order 240, the derivative residual was `3.18e-10` on the front pole and `2.39e-10` on the rear pole. Raising the source-factor order to 256 failed closed with `NUMERIC_RANGE` because the spherical Neumann recurrence became unrepresentable. This failure is retained; the axial derivative comparison remains open pending a stable scaled Hankel/source-factor strategy and NUM-04's predeclared production error budget.
- A non-gating quadrature-sensitivity diagnostic evaluated the corrected source factors through order 24 at both ends of the contract gap range, using the DEC-002 sphere radius 25 mm, piston radius 10 mm, `f=25.23 kHz`, and `c=346 m/s`. For orders 0–16, the largest successive relative changes for quadrature orders 16→32, 32→64, and 64→128 were respectively `1.14e-14`, `1.31e-14`, and `5.59e-15` at `H=0.1 mm`, and `3.35e-15`, `3.80e-15`, and `5.55e-16` at `H=30 mm`. At the near endpoint, `|f_24|=1931.45` while `|f_16|=0.81455`; the stationary coefficient-weighted magnitude for `n=24` is `1.31e-8`. At the far endpoint, `|f_24|=0.06542`. These are source-factor and coefficient diagnostics only; no field sum, truncation acceptance, or physical result follows.
- **Preserved source-integral failure and correction:** the implementation first published in `00f28f0` used `2 h_n^(1)(z)P_n(kr0/z)/z` instead of the conjugated Eq. 2 integrand `z h_n^(1)(z)P_n(kr0/z)` from [Hasegawa et al. (1985)](https://doi.org/10.1250/ast.6.9). Its Simpson test repeated the implementation's wrong integrand, so it was self-consistency evidence only. The endpoint sensitivity numbers published in `958189e` were also computed from that wrong integral. They remain historical failed results and are superseded by the corrected values above. Eq. 4's `f_0` and `f_1` closed forms now independently check the corrected kernel. No coupled field was evaluated or published with the faulty factors.

This establishes reference-code behavior in those checks, not agreement with an experimental field or the not-yet-built Hasegawa evaluator.

## Limits and open verification

These checks establish neither Hasegawa-series convergence nor accuracy across the frozen `ka`, `kR`, and gap range. The selected point comparisons check source normalization and off-axis derivatives, but do not establish broad field convergence or a production truncation tolerance. The failed near-gap axial derivative refinement and high-order Neumann overflow are unresolved. Complex Hankel coefficient conditioning in the coupled series, comparison with the plane-wave reference, full summed-field boundary behavior, P3 overlap, broader piston-only maps, axial derivative recovery, the far-field limit, and measured air data remain open. The coupled evaluator, actual live-array memory audit, preflight gate, and diagnostic pilot remain required before NUM-03 can close.

## Scope reviewed

This review covers the initial `evaluate_hasegawa_piston_sphere_field` implementation in `src/aura/fields/hasegawa.py` and its explicit-order diagnostic. The modeled case uses a coaxial uniformly moving circular piston at `z=0` and a stationary rigid sound-hard sphere centered on `+z`, in homogeneous, linear, lossless air under `exp(-iwt)`. Source factors are the conjugated Hasegawa series for the project convention. The sphere scattering branch is `-j'_n(ka)/h'_n(ka)` at every order, including stationary `n=1`; the translating-sphere branch is not used.

The routine accepts a caller-selected maximum order, piston peak face velocity, geometry, air parameters, exterior Cartesian points and a local workspace cap. It returns FIELD-1.0 pressure, particle velocity and pressure gradient. It rejects points on/behind the piston-baffle plane, points inside the sphere, points at or beyond the source-series radius `r=d`, `kr > 40`, orders above 512 and inadequate local workspace. Its order-sequence function reports observed relative changes only; it does not accept convergence. Neither interface is connected yet to NUM-02 run-level preflight, chunk manifests or the immutable run lifecycle. Runtime readiness remains INDETERMINATE.

### Direct Rayleigh-disk modal source and minimum-gap coupled overlap

The direct source expansion follows the spherical-wave addition theorem in [DLMF §10.60(i), Eq. 10.60.1–2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.60), with the outgoing `exp(+ikr)/r` kernel formed from those two identities. The expansion condition is `r<R_s` for each source point. With the sphere center as origin and a piston at `z=-d`, a disk element at radius `s` has `R_s=sqrt(d^2+s^2)` and source polar cosine `-d/R_s`. Azimuthal averaging gives the incident velocity-potential coefficient

`q_n = i k U (2n+1) integral_0^b s h_n^(1)(k R_s) P_n(-d/R_s) ds`.

The 10 mm piston and centered rigid-sphere setup match the source geometry in Hasegawa et al. (1985), [DOI 10.1250/ast.6.9](https://doi.org/10.1250/ast.6.9). The diagnostic integrates the disk-radius coefficient directly with 120-digit Decimal Gauss-Legendre arithmetic. It then applies `B_n=-j'_n(ka)/h_n^(1)'(ka)` and sums `q_n [j_n(kr)+B_n h_n^(1)(kr)] P_n(cos(theta))`; the pressure, particle velocity and pressure gradient use FIELD-1.0's `exp(-iwt)` convention. Its Bessel and derivative recurrences are implemented in the diagnostic, not imported from the public source-factor or sphere-response helpers. The comparison is therefore a separate numerical implementation of the same stated model, not a separate physical model or experimental reference.

At the frozen minimum gap `H=0.1 mm`, the evaluation covers polar angles 5°–185° at 5° increments and radii `a`, `a+H/2`, and `a+0.99H` (111 points, all within `a<=r<d`). ### Tail-bound route and domain limitation — 2026-10-04

The source-integral representation provides a candidate route for a conservative bound, but does not yet close the tail. Hasegawa et al. ([1985, Eq. (2)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ast1980/6/1/6_1_9/_article)) writes each piston source factor as an integral over `z in [kd, k*sqrt(d^2+R^2)]` with integrand `z h_n^(2)(z) P_n(kd/z)`. For real arguments, `|P_n(x)|<=1` on `[-1,1]`. NIST DLMF [§10.53, Eqs. 10.53.1–10.53.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.53) gives exact power-series representations from which absolute upper envelopes for `j_n` and `y_n`, and hence `h_n`, can be derived. Pairing such a source envelope with the regular radial factor `j_n(kr)` suggests a geometric factor involving `r/d`; derivatives add polynomial-in-order factors. Whether these estimates yield a useful all-order bound for both incident and scattered terms on a selected compact set remains to be derived and checked.

The public field domain only requires `r<d`; it has no fixed clearance `d-r>0`. Consequently `sup(r/d)` over that domain is one. The elementary absolute-value route therefore does not immediately give one uniform geometric majorant for the entire declared domain, and differentiating the series makes this limitation especially relevant to the pressure-gradient observable. This is a limitation of the current bound route, not evidence that the field diverges or that the physical model fails. A next derivation may target a compact subdomain `a<=r<=d-delta` with a stated positive `delta`, or a finite benchmark set whose minimum clearance is explicit. That would define the error claim's scope; it would not silently amend the solver's accepted domain. The scattered contribution, exact constants, all-order ratio, outward-rounded arithmetic and independent verification still need to be worked out before any tail bound can be reported.

The immediate method task is therefore to determine whether a reproducible all-order majorant can be completed on an explicitly bounded subdomain and whether its resulting pressure, velocity and gradient bounds are useful at the current order cap. If the intended accuracy claim must cover every point arbitrarily close to `r=d`, the current compact-domain route cannot substantiate it without an additional argument. Keep the scientific verdict **INDETERMINATE** until that distinction and the bound are resolved.

The next step is a source-grounded derivation and independent check of a bound for the pressure, velocity and pressure-gradient tails over the declared domain, or a documented conclusion that this series cannot provide such a bound with the available validated methods. Only after the reference uncertainty and benchmark-specific protocol are frozen may the broad NUM-04 campaign begin. Finite-order extensions and additional sparse samples alone do not close this gate.

## Delivered intermediate component

`src/aura/fields/numerical.py` provides binary64 spherical Bessel/Legendre values and derivatives, Gauss-Legendre nodes/weights, the stationary sound-hard sphere coefficient, and Hasegawa source factors `f_n` using the conjugated Eq. 2 integrand. A source-factor formula error in the first implementation was caught by direct inspection of the primary paper; the original incorrect Simpson self-comparison and its sensitivity outputs are preserved as failed evidence in the [review](../reviews/NUM-03-numerical-primitives.md). Corrected `f_0` and `f_1` checks use both composite Simpson quadrature and the paper's Eq. 4 closed forms. A later order-96 Miller-normalization overflow was corrected with scaled normalization and checked against the independent Decimal series. A separate scaled `y_n` recurrence now represents values beyond binary64 as mantissa/exponent pairs; its order-256 value at `x=11.45` matches a 160-digit Decimal recurrence. This helper is not yet used by source-factor integration or sphere-scattering products. Four axial cases compare source-only pressure with the exact Rayleigh-disk integral; six off-axis cases compare pressure, velocity and pressure gradients at three sphere-surface angles for both gap endpoints. A Decimal implementation of the conjugated Hasegawa Eq. 3 recurrence, initialized with Eqs. 4, now compares source factors with the Gauss-Legendre integral through order 24 and evaluates the observable-weighted axial derivative through order 300 at both gap endpoints. At the minimum gap, residuals at orders 253/280/300 are `1.912e-10`/`2.083e-12`/`5.537e-12` (front) and `8.536e-11`/`1.258e-11`/`3.415e-13` (rear). This shows substantial modal-tail contribution at order 253, while the nonmonotone front result prevents using a single last term as a bound. The new `_scaled_source_diffraction_coefficients` helper retains source factors as mantissa/exponent pairs through order 300; near-gap `f_280` has exponent 1127 and agrees with the Decimal recurrence. `_scaled_axial_source_derivative_terms` combines those factors with scaled regular derivatives before conversion. Across a nine-point gap grid and both poles, maximum relative residuals at orders 253/280/300 are `1.912e-10`/`1.258e-11`/`5.537e-12`; the sparse grid is not a continuous-domain bound or production truncation criterion. The older integral API still fails closed at order 256. Scaled pressure and off-axis derivative observables remain to be checked before coupled-field construction. The point checks do not establish broad piston-only field convergence or prescribe the production truncation tolerance. `src/aura/fields/_plane_sphere_reference.py` separately evaluates the truncated plane-wave/rigid-sphere partial-wave reference for pressure, velocity and pressure gradient under FIELD-1.0's phasor convention. It shares the checked special-function kernels but not the Hasegawa piston source integral. No coupled piston/sphere evaluator exists and no air-case field data has been produced.

The focused tests compare Bessel values against separate high-precision Decimal series through order 96, including five selected orders through 32 at argument `446.664123` against 340-digit values and derivatives, plus an order-1 value/derivative check at argument 2279 against 1,400-digit series. These were added after a far-exterior probe exposed low-order Miller-normalization loss. Upward recurrence is now selected when all requested orders are safely in the oscillatory region; a hard `x <= 8192` argument bound preserves the recurrence-workspace contract. These selected high-argument tests do not establish the full order/argument domain, and large-order Neumann overflow remains unresolved. Other checks cover derivative/recurrence identities, Legendre parity and finite differences, Gauss-Legendre polynomial moments and stationary-sphere modal boundary cancellation, the stationary-sphere coefficient through order 16 against a separate high-precision reference at frozen air `ka`, the first two source factors against independent Simpson quadrature and Hasegawa's Eq. 4 closed forms, scalar axial source pressure against the Rayleigh disk integral at both gap endpoints and both sphere poles, and off-axis pressure, velocity and pressure gradients against independent disk-surface quadrature at three angles and both gap endpoints. A new Decimal recurrence diagnostic checks the conjugated Hasegawa Eq. 3 against the source-factor quadrature through order 24 and the observable-weighted axial derivative against the closed-form Rayleigh derivative through order 300 at the near-gap poles. It finds residuals of `1.912e-10` (front) and `8.536e-11` (rear) at order 253, `2.083e-12` and `1.258e-11` at order 280, and `5.537e-12` and `3.415e-13` at order 300. This confirms that modal tail, rather than a small last term, controls the order-253 discrepancy at this single geometry; the nonmonotone front residual and unscaled order-256 failure remain. The exploratory `2e-11` target is not a production truncation criterion, and gap-wide convergence remains untested. The tests also exercise invalid-domain/workspace and unrepresentable-output failures. The plane-wave/rigid-sphere reference has separate boundary, phasor, small-sphere-limit and resource-gate tests. Non-gating diagnostics record source-integral quadrature sensitivity; none of these checks sets a production field truncation tolerance. A cross-component test confirms NUM-02's solver-free workspace estimate covers the source-factor helper's local peak estimate when given matched order, quadrature and Bessel-argument dimensions. These are component checks only; the new diagnostic informs but does not close the higher-order source-factor implementation or coupled evaluator validation.

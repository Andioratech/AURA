## Current conditional-method update — 2026-10-03

The NUM-01 literature screen examined the primary angular-spectrum/partial-wave method of Sapozhnikov and Bailey (2013). Its published Eq. (19) explicitly omits evanescent components. Therefore it does not by itself establish near-field pressure/velocity accuracy at the air-case minimum `0.1 mm` gap (`H/λ ≈ 0.0073`); a modified implementation would require an explicit evanescent formulation and independent near-gap convergence checks. This is documented in [NUM-01](../research/NUM-01-method-comparison.md), with the air route selected by DEC-005 and Hasegawa et al.’s centered piston/sphere series selected for P4 subject to preflight. At this checkpoint dated 2026-10-03, solver implementation had not started; the current status is recorded at the top of this file.

For the source's 20 mm circular transducer face and 50 mm sphere, under an ideal planar, coaxial-face assumption, local clearance is `g(r)=H+a−√(a²−r²)`. At `H=0.1 mm`, the sphere adds another 0.1 mm of clearance at radius `≈2.234 mm`; clearance at the 10 mm aperture edge is `≈2.187 mm`. These are conditional geometric calculations, not measured face geometry or a solver result. NUM-01 records this as a near-gap refinement scale without selecting a method.

## Solid-sphere force prior-art follow-up — 2026-10-03

The primary *Physical Review Applied* paper by Ghanem et al. (PW-10) validates an arbitrary-beam scattering model against plate-supported lateral-force measurements on 2–6 mm glass, brass and ceramic spheres in water, then steers a 5 mm sphere along a 2D path on the plate. Its abstract's approximately 22% figure is average discrepancy in the maximum trapping angle; the full text reports inferred-force discrepancy of `32.8±58.1%` overall and `11.7±8.3%` only in the subset with friction-to-acoustic-force ratio `≤0.6`. It also reports lateral force comparable to sphere weight up to 90 mg near 10 W. This prior art narrows novelty claims about solid-body force modeling, force comparable to weight and 2D steering. Plate-supported motion does not establish free 3D prescribed acceleration, general gravity-like response or AURA performance. The bounded literature search remains non-exhaustive, and no target-specific novelty claim is made. The cited Quality and no-core statements describe the 2026-10-03 checkpoint; the present NUM-03 implementation has its own validation status above.

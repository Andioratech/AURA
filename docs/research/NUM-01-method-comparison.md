## Decision boundary

LIT-02 retains SRC-W03/MQ1 as an exploratory reference; raw measured velocity fields and complete source, wall, calibration, and uncertainty inputs are unavailable in this checkout. No currently reviewed method can therefore be selected as a reproducible model of the published device.

The first numerical task can still use an explicitly manufactured domain. ## Evidence-derived MQ1 constraints

The primary source describes a silicon-glass chip with a water channel of length 35 mm, width 377 µm, and height 157 µm. Figure 1 centers the transverse coordinate, with channel edges at `y = ±w/2`; under that convention its ideal field `p₁(r) = pₐ cos[k(y − w/2)]`, with `k = π/w`, has a pressure node at `y = 0` and zero normal pressure gradient at both channel edges. MQ1 is listed at 1.940 MHz. The same source lists water at 25 °C with `c₀ = 1497 m/s`, and states that boundary-driven streaming depends on the rectangular walls and thermoviscous effects. See Barnkob et al., [author manuscript, Figure 1 and Sections II–IV, Eqs. (1), (2), (6)–(15), and Table II](https://arxiv.org/html/1208.6534).

These reported quantities do not close under a homogeneous-water, exact half-wave calculation:

| Calculation | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| `c₀ / f` using 1497 m/s and 1.940 MHz | 771.65 µm wavelength | Conditional homogeneous-water wavelength |
| `c₀ / (2f)` | 385.82 µm half wavelength | 8.82 µm (2.34%) above the reported 377 µm width |
| `2 f w` | 1462.76 m/s implied speed for exact half-wave | 34.24 m/s (2.29%) below the reported `c₀` |
| `w / (c₀/f)` | 0.4886 | Reported width is not exactly one half of this conditional wavelength |

This arithmetic is not evidence of an error in the experiment. It identifies unresolved effective resonance, geometry, and material inputs. Do not silently substitute an adjusted sound speed or frequency. The article's ideal cosine with the figure's centered coordinate remains a valid manufactured reference under its own declared half-wave assumption, but matching it is not a reconstruction of the resonant silicon-glass device.

The article further reports that its measured symmetry-plane particle velocity combines radiation-force response and acoustic-streaming advection; the two mechanisms vary with particle size. It states that the simple parallel-plate streaming approximation is modified by rectangular side walls and thermoviscous effects. Thus MQ1 velocity is not a direct acoustic-force or acceleration measurement. The raw field-data and calibration gaps recorded in [LIT-02](../benchmarks/water-reference-selection.md) remain unchanged.

The BEM review by van ’t Wout et al. (2021) describes acoustic Helmholtz transmission systems as dense after boundary discretization, notes the need for compression/preconditioning at scale, and emphasizes that no single formulation wins across all frequencies, materials, and geometries; see the [primary author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04594). This supports treating BEM as a later qualified branch rather than assuming that “BEM” names one universal solver. k-Wave's official [PML description](https://www.k-wave.org/documentation/example_na_controlling_the_pml.php) explains that its layer absorbs waves at computational edges and prevents FFT wraparound; that boundary behavior must not be mistaken for a reflecting microchannel wall.

## Dependency and license observations

The current ENV-1.0 lock contains neither NumPy nor SciPy. A Python finite-difference eigenmode prototype using sparse linear algebra would add both dependencies and require platform wheels, exact versions, transitive lock hashes, and wheel-bundled license notices to be reviewed before adoption. The NumPy and SciPy upstream projects identify BSD-3-Clause licensing ([NumPy license](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/license), [SciPy license](https://github.com/scipy/scipy/blob/main/LICENSE.txt)); this does not replace a review of the exact installed distributions. The official k-Wave MATLAB toolbox and the separate `k-wave-python` project report LGPL-3.0; their runtime and implementation paths differ, and a Python wrapper does not itself supply missing chip boundary conditions. See the [official k-Wave repository license notice](https://github.com/ucl-bug/k-wave/blob/main/README.md) and [k-wave-python repository](https://github.com/waltsims/k-wave-python). No solver package or dependency has been selected.

For the periodic streamwise coordinate and Neumann transverse interval, the separated spectrum is `k² = (2πm/Lₓ)² + (nπ/w)²`, with nonnegative integer mode indices. The constant `(m,n) = (0,0)` is the zero eigenvalue. With `Lₓ = 892 µm` and `w = 377 µm`, the first streamwise wavenumber is `kₓ₁ = 2π/Lₓ`, while the target first transverse wavenumber is `kᵧ₁ = π/w`; their ratio is `kₓ₁/kᵧ₁ = 2w/Lₓ = 377/446 ≈ 0.84529`. Thus the `(1,0)` streamwise mode has lower positive `k` than the target `(0,1)` transverse mode. An eigensolver must request the target symmetry/subspace or identify modes by both indices; selecting only the smallest nonzero eigenvalue would answer a different question.

This domain is a deliberate manufactured overlap: it adds an independent discretized method while retaining an exact analytical comparison. The ideal sound-hard walls define the oracle problem; they do not model the physical silicon-glass chip's elastic response. If the next project question requires the *measured MQ1 field*, this recommendation is inadequate; obtain the source supplement and calibrated transducer/wall information, then revisit the boundary/scattering representation and a coupled FEM/BEM or validated alternative. Do not use this mode as a surrogate for the physical drive.

## Preliminary resource envelope

Observed in this VM on 2026-10-03: Linux x86_64, 4 exposed CPUs, approximately 7.76 GiB total memory with no swap, approximately 6.10 GiB available at observation, and approximately 219.5 GiB free on the checkout filesystem. These are transient machine observations, not project-wide guarantees.

For the proposed 2D finite-difference eigenproblem, use three illustrative grids with 33, 65, and 129 transverse nodes and approximately isotropic spacing over the 892 µm streamwise span: at most `N = 39,345` nodes (`305 × 129`) on the finest grid. A five-point matrix then has fewer than `5N = 196,725` nonzeros. With float64 values and 32-bit sparse indices, CSR storage is under 3 MiB; a 20-vector complex iterative workspace is under 13 MiB. The required raw field arrays in FIELD-1.0—`[N,3]` float64 coordinates, `[N]` complex128 pressure, and `[N,3]` complex128 velocity and gradient—need 136 bytes per sample, or 5,350,920 bytes (about 5.10 MiB) at `N=39,345`. If a derived `[N,3]` float64 mean-intensity array is also retained, add 24 bytes per sample for 6,295,200 bytes (about 6.00 MiB) total before metadata/checksums. These are raw-array lower bounds, not predictions for the JSON/run-bundle encoding; measure actual serialization and peak process memory during NUM-06 and protect both with NUM-02 headroom. Python, SciPy, factorization/eigensolver workspace and allocator overhead also remain unmeasured. No runtime is predicted from these counts alone.

Proposed implementation guardrails for owner review are 39,345 grid nodes, 0.5 GiB process-memory cap, 64 MiB output cap, and 120 seconds wall time per eigen-solve on the documented local CPU profile. They are conservative operational starting caps, not measured performance or scientific tolerances. NUM-02 must refine peak-memory/runtime estimates, expose user-configurable caps with safety headroom, and reject an over-budget request before allocating solver arrays. The observed VM is enough for the proposed small case; a GPU or remote machine is not justified at this stage.

## Conditional technical scope if P4 is aligned with the DEC-002 air case

This subsection scopes the air alternative for the owner's comparison; it does not select it or change the approved baseline. Andrade et al. (2016) calculate the single-transducer/sphere field with a 2D axisymmetric linear-acoustic FEM model: a prescribed uniform harmonic displacement on the circular transducer face, an acoustically hard sphere, and PMLs at the outer air-domain boundaries. They obtain first-order pressure and velocity, then integrate the time-averaged momentum flux over a closed surface around the sphere to calculate axial radiation force. Their source computation is therefore a useful method/domain description, not an independent validation target for another implementation. The owner-approved experimental observable remains the measured force-versus-gap curve; its uncertainty status and P1.3 INDETERMINATE rule are unchanged.

For the declared `f = 25,230 Hz`, `c₀ = 346 m/s`, and sphere radius `a = 25 mm`, `λ = c₀/f ≈ 13.714 mm` and `ka = 2πfa/c₀ ≈ 11.45` (`a/λ ≈ 1.823`). This is a wavelength-scale scattering problem, not a Rayleigh/Gor'kov small-particle case. The 10 mm source-face radius is also about `0.73λ`, so its finite-aperture field cannot be replaced by a plane wave without a separately justified approximation. The experimental gap range starts at `0.1 mm`, about `0.0073λ`; a candidate method must demonstrate that its geometry representation and near-boundary quadrature remain accurate there rather than relying on a coarse uniform mesh.

There is also an evidence-coverage mismatch: the primary article describes its axisymmetric FEM pressure/force results over `H = 2–30 mm`, while its experiment and AURA's approved P1.3 reference span `H = 0.1–30 mm`. The checked derived CSVs contain 300 experimental samples; 19 points from `0.1–1.9 mm` lie outside the article's stated FEM interval, and the repository has digitized only the experimental (red) trace. These points are not source-FEM comparisons. An air-route solver intended to support the full later P1.3 comparison must establish its own numerical validity in this small-gap region; P4 itself still verifies fields only. Details and the no-omission rule are recorded in the [P1.3 specification](../benchmarks/P1.3-andrade-force-curve-specification.md#published-simulation-coverage-versus-measured-coverage).

An additional primary-literature method was screened but is not a direct third candidate for this P4 scope. Sapozhnikov and Bailey (2013) decompose a known incident pressure plane into plane-wave angular-spectrum components, scatter each component from a sphere by partial waves, and calculate radiation force from the far-field stress. Their Eq. (19) explicitly restricts the spectrum to `kₓ² + kᵧ² ≤ k²`, neglecting evanescent waves. This is a published force formulation for an ideal inviscid fluid, not a verified near-field pressure/velocity solver for the source-and-sphere gap. At the minimum `H = 0.1 mm` gap (`H/λ ≈ 0.0073`), AURA cannot assume the omitted evanescent content is negligible. A modified angular-spectrum/partial-wave implementation would first need an explicitly derived evanescent treatment and independent near-gap convergence checks before reconsideration; no such extension is selected or implemented. See the [primary JASA article, especially Eqs. (18)–(20)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3574112/).

The rigid-sphere partial-wave check is an ideal verification problem, not a model of the published foam sphere: the article itself adopts an acoustically hard boundary, while the experimental specimen's material response is not fully characterized in the source record. The exact plane-wave sphere solution checks scattering and spatial field accuracy but does not verify the nearby finite piston. A piston-only analytical integral and the P3 plane-wave cases would test those pieces separately before the coupled geometry is attempted. The original paper's COMSOL/FEM output may be included as a published numerical comparison, but it is neither the experimental force curve nor an independent solver oracle. The experimental figure contains force-versus-gap data, not measured pressure/velocity maps; therefore P4 can establish numerical verification in declared ideal air configurations, but cannot claim measured-field validation. Force integration and comparison with the published force curve belong to the later force-model gate, where the P1.3 uncertainty rule still limits any formal outcome to INDETERMINATE unless authoritative uncertainty becomes available. P4 must not claim that it has validated the force or AURA's prescribed-acceleration objective.

Primary references for this conditional scope: Andrade, Bernassau & Adamowski (2016), [peer-reviewed article](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4959862) and [Heriot-Watt author manuscript](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/acoustic-levitation-of-a-large-solid-sphere/); the rigid-sphere exact-series numerical warning, ["Exact evaluation of an ultrasonic scattering formula for a rigid immovable sphere" (1981)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0301-5629(81)90040-5); and the existing formulation-dependent BEM comparison by van ’t Wout et al. (2021), cited below. These sources scope candidate verification cases only; they do not establish a validated AURA method.

## Recommendation and alternatives

**Recommendation:** if the owner accepts a manufactured numerical-verification subproblem as the first P4 target, use a CPU finite-difference eigenmode solver for the ideal 2D Neumann/periodic domain above, explicitly targeting `(mₓ,nᵧ) = (0,1)`. Require a full-field overlap with the matched P3 counterpropagating-wave solution for pressure, velocity, gradient and mean intensity; retain the closed-form eigenvalue/profile as a second reference. This is independent of FEM, has closed-form discriminating references, is small enough for local preflight, and avoids inventing an MQ1 transducer drive. The sound-hard boundaries are only the stated ideal boundary condition; they do not establish the physical chip response. Keep the existing analytical kernels as the B-03…B-06 regression baseline. Treat angular spectrum or k-space as later propagation alternatives only when a measured source plane, open-domain requirement, or heterogeneity justifies them. Reserve coupled FEM/BEM for a separately specified fluid-solid or scattering question.

**Alternative A:** defer NUM-03 until the original numerical MQ1 fields, source calibration and chip-boundary data are available; then choose a solver for that measured domain. The public thesis-hosted facsimile and later `FIG-W03-MQ1-20261003-01` artifact provide acquisition metadata, plots and six figure-derived one-dimensional axial-average profiles. They still provide neither the original numerical 79 × 39 fields, pointwise uncertainty/covariance, calibration nor boundary inputs. The extracted profiles cannot substitute for source fields in a solver comparison or justify a formal measurement fit/tolerance. The direct APS supplement route remains subscription-gated and returned HTTP 401 unauthenticated; owner/institutional APS access is untested. See the [access follow-up](../benchmarks/water-reference-selection.md#post-lit-02-access-follow-up) and [extraction review](../reviews/FIG-W03-MQ1-profile-extraction.md). Formal selection remains INDETERMINATE.

**Alternative B:** restrict the next task to homogeneous free-space transducer/array propagation with a declared measured or manufactured aperture field. Analytical superposition or angular spectrum is fast and simple, but this changes the initial numerical question away from the published channel resonance and must not be represented as an MQ1 reproduction.

No method is frozen, no dependency was installed, and no simulation code or result was created. Please select one route before NUM-02/NUM-03. The decision changes what the first numerical backend means scientifically, so implementation stops here pending that owner choice.

# Assessment of the AURA Scientific-Risk Critique

**Prepared:** 2026-10-03 · **Purpose:** source-grounded assessment of the owner's nine-point critique · **Status:** assessment only; it does not change project scope, gate status or acceptance criteria

## Overall finding

The critique identifies real scientific and project risks. Its strongest points are the need to define any “pseudogravity” claim operationally, separate body-specific force/acceleration from gravity equivalence, measure open-loop field/body-force quality separately from feedback performance, test robustness against evidenced uncertainties, and keep feasibility distinct from usefulness. AURA's D00/D01 and plans already encode several of these boundaries, but they do not yet freeze a target-specific acceleration acceptance metric or claim hierarchy.

Two parts need precision. First, a force that depends on position does not make prescribed acceleration logically impossible: a designed field can have an approximately constant force over a declared region, and a controller may compensate residual variation. Those are different results that must be reported separately. Second, “force requires gradients” is a useful shorthand for some small-particle potential models and traps, not a universal statement across body sizes and scattering regimes. A general rigid-body calculation must account for incident-plus-scattered fields and momentum/radiation stress on a declared surface.

## Point-by-point assessment

| Critique point | Assessment | Evidence and project consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Controlled acoustic force is not automatically gravity or general pseudogravity | **Correct; central claim boundary.** | D00 defines acoustic pseudogravity as mechanically induced acceleration and says it is not gravity generation; D01 distinguishes body/material/geometry-dependent acoustic forces from gravitational coupling. The claim must be scoped to the declared body and operating domain. A single body's acceleration does not establish body-independent gravity-like response. See [D00](../D00-document-control.md) and [D01](../D01-system-architecture.md). |
| 2. Field nonuniformity may make sustained acceleration difficult | **Valid engineering/science risk; not a contradiction or universal impossibility.** | Inoue et al.'s 40 kHz air experiment reports that each 30 mm sphere's surrounding field changed significantly when the sphere was introduced. Their measured restoring-force maps are position-dependent; five of ten starts more than 5 mm from the levitation point fell within 4 s, while the other five trials remained levitated for 50–144 s. The authors identify a roughly 5 mm stable region and note that perfect asymptotic stability was not reached under their model, which neglected air viscosity. This supports body-in-field characterization in that specific setup; it is not a universal volume limit or evidence about water particles/AURA. It does not prove that every field is too nonuniform: a later case could demonstrate near-constant force over a registered workspace or compensate variation. The small-particle potential-gradient intuition must not be generalized to every scattering regime. Use surface force/torque versus body pose and report both open-loop variation and closed-loop tracking. See the primary paper's Figs. 2–4 and Methods, especially the stated sound-hard-boundary and inviscid-air assumptions ([full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.05988v1)). |
| 3. Choose a precise initial object and target | **Correct for each falsifiable campaign; do not shrink the whole program to that campaign.** | The target/body/material/medium/environment/trajectory/duration/workspace/observation contract is still open (UNK-003–013). The suggested 0.1–1 g, dimensions and volume are illustrative only, not approved values. DEC-004 explicitly retains larger masses, dimensions and geometries as later research. Freeze one case per campaign while preserving the staged scale track. See [DEC-004](../decisions/DEC-004-staged-mass-and-size-expansion.md), [scale progression](../planning/11-scale-progression.md) and [UNK-011](../registers/unknowns.md). |
| 4. Simulation needs uncertainty/perturbation tests | **Correct, with sequencing and provenance qualifications.** | Numerical verification and nominal model checks come before robustness conclusions. Perturbation levels such as ±5% amplitude are not evidence-based by themselves; derive intervals/distributions from calibration, datasheets or measurement, and label hypothetical stress tests as such. Add them to the appropriate P8 attack/holdout protocol after the model and observable are frozen. Robustness does not replace independent model checks or measurement validation. See [P8](../planning/phases/P08-adversarial-limits.md) and UNK-005/008/012. |
| 5. Separate field quality from closed-loop performance | **Correct and directly useful.** | A controller can track a target while compensating a poor or spatially varying field. Record the open-loop field and body-force/torque map for the declared case, then closed-loop trajectory error, actuation and saturation separately. “Field quality” must be an operational statistic, not a visual judgment from pressure plots. For a finite body, surface loading, net force and torque may matter more than pressure maps alone. See [D03](../D03-requirements.md), [P7](../planning/phases/P07-target-control.md) and SC-02's model review. |
| 6. Novelty needs sharper comparison | **Correct; no novelty claim is presently warranted.** | In addition to air phased-array manipulation and reduced-gravity trapping, Matouš et al. demonstrated video-feedback position regulation and moving-reference trajectory following of a small ball with an 8×8 ultrasonic array; their paper describes a 50 Hz loop and up to 10 mm/s tracking in the floating-ball experiment. This rules out generic feedback-controlled acoustic trajectory manipulation as an AURA novelty claim. Their planar, surface-supported setup does not resolve whether an acceleration-targeted, traceable field-to-force model for a separately frozen bulk-fluid or microgravity body/domain is novel. That possible distinction remains a search question, not a finding. See [prior-work register](../registers/prior-work.md) and the [primary IFAC paper/preprint](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2019.11.722). |
| 7. Add “g-error” | **Useful candidate metric, but incomplete until defined and paired with uncertainty.** | One candidate is vector acceleration RMSE over a declared evaluation window: `a_RMS = sqrt((1/T) ∫ ||a_meas(t)-a_target(t)||² dt)`. If reporting in g, define the reference `g_ref` and use `ε_g=a_RMS/g_ref`; do not use “g” as a unit without a convention. Also report vector bias, peak error, overshoot/settling only where the response has a justified target, spatial/workspace coverage, uncertainty and the observation method. Acceleration inferred from noisy position is itself an uncertainty-sensitive measurement. This is proposed, not adopted; freeze it in CTL-01 before a target run. |
| 8. Separate “can it be done?” from efficiency/usefulness | **Correct.** | Establish force/trajectory feasibility for a frozen target independently from power, energy, heating, transducer efficiency, acoustic exposure and duration constraints. Distinguish electrical input, acoustic power delivered to a field and mechanical power on the body; stationary holding has zero instantaneous mechanical power `F·v` while electrical draw and heating may continue. Do not use a power-only estimate as a universal force bound. See [LIT-05 limit design](limits-design.md) and D02. |
| 9. Scope expansion can undermine the research | **Valid project-management risk; resolve through gated work, not by discarding the owner's approved broader objective.** | The work board and DEC-004 already separate an initial water-particle campaign from greater-mass, larger-size and shape work. Keep each as a bounded branch with its own model/evidence contract; do not introduce arrays, adaptation, multiple bodies or application claims without an approved task and discriminating question. |

## Proposed operational claim ladder

These are distinctions for future claim review, not a change to D00:

1. **Object-specific acoustic acceleration:** the measured/predicted center-of-mass acceleration of a specified body under specified conditions meets a declared target statistic for a declared time window.
2. **Robust object-specific acceleration:** the same criterion holds across a predeclared uncertainty set and held-out disturbances supported by evidence.
3. **Gravity-like response over a test class/region:** multiple bodies or positions respond within a declared equivalence tolerance and spatial/time domain. This requires a separate operational test; one-object tracking cannot establish it.
4. **Gravity generation:** not an AURA claim. Mechanical acoustic forcing does not create gravitational interaction.

The word “pseudogravity” may be retained as a project label only with the D00 definition and the selected claim level attached. It must not replace the measurable statement in a result.

## Changes warranted now

- Add UNK-016 for the still-unfrozen operational acceleration/pseudogravity acceptance contract. D00/D01 provide the conceptual boundary; CTL-01 must define target, metric, frame, evaluation window, workspace and uncertainty before any target run.
- Preserve field quality versus control performance as separate future observables; no implementation or metric threshold is set here.
- Use evidence-derived uncertainty in P8; do not adopt the critique's example perturbations as defaults.
- Keep the P3 result bounded to ideal analytical field software verification. These concerns do not invalidate P3 evidence, but they remain prerequisites for later force, motion, target and robustness claims.

No equations, solver contracts, numeric limits, D09 claim status or phase approval are changed by this assessment.

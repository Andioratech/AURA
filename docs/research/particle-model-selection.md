# Small-Particle Force Model Selection and Applicability

**Prepared:** 2026-10-03 · **Work item:** LIT-03 · **Status:** literature comparison complete; no force law implemented or experimentally validated

## Bounded question

Which published small-sphere acoustic radiation-force formulations are defensible candidates for the SRC-W03 exploratory MQ1 domain (polystyrene particles in water, 25 °C, nominal 1.940 MHz transverse half-wave channel), and which regime checks must precede implementation?

The result concerns the *radiation force* on an isolated spherical particle under a time-periodic acoustic field. It does not by itself predict the measured particle velocity, which in SRC-W03 includes external boundary-driven streaming, viscous drag, channel-wall effects and thermoviscous effects. It does not cover the full microchannel boundary-value problem, dense suspensions, arbitrary shapes, or AURA's larger-object targets.

The articles use `exp(-iωt)` in the selected convention. SRC-W01 Eq. (14) expresses force through a surrounding fixed surface's second-order pressure and momentum-flux terms (in its inviscid outer-fluid evaluation). SRC-W02 Eqs. (1)–(4) define the averaged stress force and its surrounding-surface momentum-flux form; Eq. (5) reduces this to first-order incident fields and monopole/dipole scattering coefficients. Sign, normal direction, peak-phasor averaging, and particle versus fluid stress conventions must be carried into any independent implementation.

## Applicability indicators for SRC-W03 MQ1

Source-matched inputs are Barnkob et al.'s measured MQ1 frequency 1.940 MHz, water values at 25 °C (`ρ0 = 997 kg m⁻³`, `c0 = 1497 m s⁻¹`, `η = 0.890 mPa s`, `δs = 0.38 µm`), particle diameters `(0.59 ± 0.03), (0.99 ± 0.05), (1.91 ± 0.07), (2.57 ± 0.07), (5.11 ± 0.16), (10.16 ± 0.20) µm`, and channel dimensions 35 mm × 377 µm × 157 µm. Particle-size uncertainty types differ by row: some sizes are Coulter measurements; the 0.59 µm value comes from manufacturer data with an assumed 5% standard deviation. The article's raw acquisition supplement remains unavailable in this checkout.

## Model comparison and bounded choice

| Model branch | Strength in MQ1 domain | Limitation / trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Inviscid Gor'kov/Yosioka-Kawasima long-wavelength sphere | Compact radiation-force reference in an ideal fluid; useful as a limiting check when viscous and thermal boundary effects are negligible. | Not justified as the sole reference for the 0.59–2.57 µm sizes where `δs/a` is about 0.30–1.29; its assumptions omit the viscous particle boundary layer. Do not select from `ka` alone. |
| SRC-W01 viscous Settnes–Bruus sphere | Primary candidate for the radiation-force term over all six MQ1 sizes: `ka ≪ 1` and its coefficient spans arbitrary `δs/a`. It directly addresses the viscous scale seen in MQ1. | Excludes heat conduction/thermal boundary corrections in the scattering coefficients, finite channel boundaries and external streaming. Requires matched solid compressibility/density, viscosity, field and wall/streaming treatment. |
| SRC-W02 thermoviscous Karlsen–Bruus sphere | Most complete of the compared radiation-force formulations for a small thermoelastic solid sphere in a thermoviscous fluid; handles arbitrary ordering of particle radius and boundary-layer thicknesses while all remain small relative to wavelength. | More inputs and derivation complexity; property set for the actual particle/fluid batch and matched field amplitude are incomplete. Its infinite, quiescent-fluid force solution does not predict channel streaming or wall-coupled fields. |

**Bounded model-selection result:** do not freeze an executable force model yet. For a future isolated radiation-force calculation in the MQ1 size/frequency domain, use SRC-W02 as the preferred *candidate formulation* for an audit of the complete thermoviscous coefficients; retain SRC-W01 as an independent viscous comparator and asymptotic/limiting check; retain the inviscid model only as a clearly labeled ideal limit. This choice is driven by the measured scale ratios, not a claim that thermal corrections dominate. Formal selection for a coupled particle-velocity benchmark waits on matched particle/fluid thermal properties, field calibration and separately treated streaming/wall effects (UNK-003…009).

Do not transfer this selection to larger bodies, other shapes, air, a finite transducer/body interaction or a different frequency without a new applicability assessment. The separate air-scale path remains acoustic scattering by the full object with appropriate boundary conditions and measured force evidence under P1.3; no particle formula is extrapolated to it.

## Future independent verification plan

Before implementation, the force contract must freeze the complex-amplitude convention, tensor/stress sign and outward normals, particle type, exact coefficient branch, material-property source, field input and excluded terms. Independent checks should include: (1) dimensional analysis of the vector-force expression and coefficient units; (2) zero contrast and zero-amplitude limits; (3) symmetry/force direction in a 1D standing wave; (4) recovery of SRC-W01 and inviscid coefficients as SRC-W02 boundary-layer limits where those limits apply; (5) direct independent numerical evaluation of the surrounding-surface momentum-flux equation on a declared surface; and (6) a separate matched measurement comparison using data not used to calibrate field amplitude. These are plans, not passed tests. Tolerances must follow numerical conditioning and source uncertainty and be reviewed before code or comparison runs.

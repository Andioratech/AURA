# P1.1: Published Benchmark Candidate Dossier

**Status:** REVIEW
**Prepared:** 2026-10-01
**Phase:** P1 — bounded scientific question and benchmark selection
**Work item:** P1.1 — extract candidate benchmarks from primary literature

## Scope

This dossier records three published candidates for a first bounded acoustic-force study. It transcribes reported conditions, observables, reference values, uncertainty, and known gaps. It does not score or select a candidate; that is P1.2–P1.3. None of these small-particle studies establishes performance for macroscopic bodies or AURA's proposed acceleration objective.

## Candidate A — viscous radiation and streaming velocities in a rectangular microchannel

**Primary source:** R. Barnkob, P. Augustsson, T. Laurell, and H. Bruus, “Acoustic radiation- and streaming-induced microparticle velocities determined by microparticle image velocimetry in an ultrasound symmetry plane,” *Physical Review E* 86, 056307 (2012), [doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.86.056307](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.86.056307). The authors' [full-text version](https://arxiv.org/html/1208.6534) is available.

- **Regime and model:** dilute spherical polystyrene particles in a resonant, one-dimensional transverse standing-wave field in a silicon/glass water microchannel. The observable combines time-averaged radiation-force drift and acoustic-streaming Stokes drag. The paper uses a small-particle viscous radiation-force expression, a streaming model, and wall-drag corrections; its Eqs. (2), (11)–(15) define the force/velocity relation and fitted profile.
- **Geometry and field:** channel length 35 mm, width 377 µm, height 157 µm; half-wave condition `lambda/2 = w` at 1.940 MHz (or full-wave `lambda = w` at 3.900 MHz). Measurements were made in the horizontal ultrasound symmetry plane. Conditions include water and a 0.75:0.25 water:glycerol mixture.
- **Material values at 25 °C:** polystyrene density 1050 kg m⁻³, compressibility 249 TPa⁻¹, contrast factor `Phi = 0.17` in water; water density 997 kg m⁻³, sound speed 1497 m s⁻¹, dynamic viscosity 0.890 mPa s, and compressibility 448 TPa⁻¹. Measured particle diameters span about 0.6–10.2 µm, with reported diameter spreads in Table 3.
- **Primary observable:** fitted transverse particle-velocity amplitude `u_a` versus particle radius/diameter, with spatial form `u_y = u_a sin(2 k y)` away from the side walls. The benchmark can also test the reported radiation-to-streaming velocity ratios in Table 5 and the fitted/endpoint parameters in Table 4.
- **Reported references:** for MQ1 (water, 1.940 MHz), Table 4(b) reports `E_ac = 32.436 ± 1.282 J m⁻³` and streaming coefficient `s = 0.182 ± 0.008`; Table 5 reports `u_a(0.6 µm)/u_a(10 µm) = 0.020` and `u_a(10 µm)/u_a(0.6 µm) = 49.6`. The diameter-scaling ratio for the 0.6, 5, and 10 µm classes is 0.294 against the idealized `(5/10)^2 = 0.25` prediction. The authors report an 8–18% deviation across series for that ratio. Table 4(a) instead fits all particle sizes and returns different `E_ac` and `s`; the two extraction methods must not be conflated.
- **Uncertainty and limitations:** particle-size dispersion and fit uncertainty are reported; the authors identify possible density/compressibility variation, non-Gaussian size distributions, concentration-driven particle interactions, and viscosity changes as uncertainty sources. The 0.6 µm and 10 µm endpoints are treated as streaming-dominated and radiation-dominated, respectively, to estimate some ratios. Flow data are micro-PIV fields and supplementary material, not a simple single force value. Wall interactions, streaming, channel boundaries, and temperature dependence make this richer but less isolated than a force-only test. Exact raw-data availability and digitization needs remain to be checked.
- **Candidate use:** strong experimental candidate for a microchannel particle-velocity benchmark and cross-check of force-plus-drag implementation; it is not a direct isolated-force measurement.

## Candidate B — three-dimensional particle trajectories and velocity field

**Primary source:** P. B. Muller et al., “Ultrasound-induced acoustophoretic motion of microparticles in three dimensions,” *Physical Review E* 88, 023006 (2013), [doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.88.023006](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.88.023006). The authors' [full-text version](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.0201) is available.

- **Regime and model:** measured 3D motion of spherical polystyrene particles in a resonant rectangular microchannel. The predictions combine radiation force and streaming-induced drag, with an analytical channel model and a numerical comparison. The reported experimental benchmark therefore tests a coupled flow-and-particle response rather than force alone.
- **Geometry and field:** silicon/glass channel length 35 mm, width 377 µm, height 157 µm; transverse half-wave resonance at 1.94 MHz. The setup was temperature-controlled. For 5 µm particles, 10 experiments yielded 111 trajectories at `U_pp = 0.91 V`; for 0.5 µm particles, four experiments yielded 731 trajectories at `U_pp = 1.62 V`.
- **Material and measurement values:** nominal particle diameters 5.33 µm (SD 0.09) and 0.537 µm (PDI 0.005); density 1050 kg m⁻³ and compressibility 249 TPa⁻¹. Reported position uncertainty is ±1 µm in `z` and less than ±0.1 µm in `x` and `y`.
- **Primary observable:** 3D trajectories and binned velocity vectors in the channel cross-section. Figure 5 compares the 0.5 µm experiment, analytical prediction, and numerical solution on a 37×15 array of 10 µm bins; Fig. 4 shows trajectories. The model's primary reported data include velocity magnitude and direction as spatial fields.
- **Reported reference values:** for the 0.5 µm case, Figure 5 reports maximum measured speed 63 µm s⁻¹ and analytical/numerical maximum 59 µm s⁻¹. The authors report experimental speeds approximately 20% higher on average than the analytical prediction; the mean experimental error is about 1 µm s⁻¹ (relative average about 4%), and the experiment and analytical prediction do not agree within the combined stated error. The acoustic energy density for the 0.5 µm case is calibrated from the 5 µm case and voltage scaling as `65 ± 2 J m⁻³`; for the 5 µm calibration, `20.6 ± 0.7 J m⁻³` is reported.
- **Uncertainty and limitations:** reported uncertainty includes position tracking, standard error of mean by bins, and uncertainty in acoustic energy-density calibration. Side-wall bins lack experimental data. Five selected near-centerline 5 µm trajectories calibrate acoustic energy density, which creates a calibration dependency between cases. A persistent ~20% experiment/model difference is a result to preserve and investigate, not a tolerance to waive. Raw numerical arrays and the availability of trajectory files need confirmation; figures alone may require digitization.
- **Candidate use:** strong candidate if a 3D particle-motion observable is desired and the first implementation is allowed to include acoustic streaming and channel-wall effects. Its complexity and the reported discrepancy may make it unsuitable as the minimal first benchmark.

## Candidate C — analytical viscous radiation force on a small sphere

**Primary source:** M. Settnes and H. Bruus, “Forces acting on a small particle in an acoustical field in a viscous fluid,” *Physical Review E* 85, 016327 (2012), [doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.85.016327](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.85.016327). The [published-paper PDF](https://bruus-lab.dk/TMF/publications/Bruus/Bruus_101.pdf) is available from the authors' research group.

- **Regime and model:** first-order scattering plus viscous acoustic-boundary-layer correction for a compressible sphere in a liquid. The long-wavelength conditions require both particle radius `a` and viscous penetration depth `delta` to be much smaller than wavelength `lambda`; the model has no restrictions on `delta/a` within that asymptotic limit. Eqs. (47)–(50) provide standing- and traveling-wave force expressions.
- **Published numerical case:** 5 µm diameter Pyrex sphere in water at 1.5 MHz under a planar traveling wave, with acoustic energy density 100 J m⁻³. The paper reports `a = 2.5 µm`, `delta/a = 0.23`, `k = 4.24 × 10³ m⁻¹`, density ratio 2.23, imaginary dipole coefficient `f₂ᵢ = 0.058`, radiation force about 1.2 pN, and terminal Stokes speed about 25 µm s⁻¹. The paper's comparison with standard inviscid theory gives a force roughly `10⁻⁶` as large and speed below 0.1 nm s⁻¹ for the cited setup.
- **Available parameters:** Table I provides water and Pyrex density, compressibility, sound speed, and viscosity. For water at 20 °C it reports `rho = 998.2 kg m⁻³`, `kappa = 456 TPa⁻¹`, `c = 1482 m s⁻¹`, `eta = 1.002 mPa s`; for Pyrex it reports `rho = 2230 kg m⁻³`, `kappa = 27.8 TPa⁻¹`, `c = 5674 m s⁻¹`.
- **Primary observable:** calculated time-averaged force along the traveling-wave vector as a function of particle radius, frequency, acoustic energy density, and `delta/a`. In a standing-wave variant, Eq. (50) defines a spatially varying force via the acoustic contrast factor.
- **Uncertainty and limitations:** the paper presents analytical theory and illustrative calculations, not a direct experimental data set or a numerical uncertainty interval. It is straightforward to reproduce its closed-form case but using the same equations as the reference would not be an independent validation. The paper discusses a sign discrepancy in a prior Doinikov expression and attributes it as possibly a misprint; this is a literature caution, not a reported erratum to Settnes and Bruus.
- **Candidate use:** a useful analytical known-answer test for a force-model implementation after the modeling regime is fixed; it cannot alone validate the physical force law against independent measurements.

## Cross-source notes and unresolved checks

- Candidate A and B have substantial author/method overlap and both depend on the same microchannel family; they should not be presented as fully independent confirmations of each other.
- Candidate A's Table 5 describes velocity ratios, not direct force measurements. Any comparison must keep the force-to-velocity conversion, Stokes drag, wall correction, and streaming contribution explicit.
- Candidate B publishes a useful discrepancy: its measured small-particle field is around 20% above the analytical mean, outside reported combined uncertainty. Preserve it as a benchmark outcome rather than adopting 20% as a project tolerance.
- Candidate C provides a clean closed-form calculation but no independent experimental output. It can be an analytical reference, not the sole published validation.
- Barnkob et al. discuss an earlier 500 kHz standing-wave experiment by K. Yasuda and T. Kamakura (1997; DOI [10.1063/1.119395](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.119395)) and state that their results do not support its reported sub-5 µm size trend. The earlier paper's full parameter set and data have not yet been checked, so it is a provenance/contradiction lead, not a scored candidate here.
- Before P1.2 scoring, inspect the relevant full-text figures, supplements, and parameter provenance directly; confirm which data are downloadable versus only plotted, and separate paper-reported uncertainty from uncertainty introduced by digitization.

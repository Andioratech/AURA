# P1.3: Andrade Sphere Force-Curve Benchmark Specification

**Status:** REVIEW — comparison rule drafted; formal source uncertainty unavailable
**Prepared:** 2026-10-02
**Phase:** P1 — bounded scientific question and benchmark selection

## Question and scope

Can an AURA force calculation reproduce the published axial force-versus-gap measurement for one 50 mm expanded-polystyrene sphere and one ultrasonic transducer in air, within the limits of the published record?

This is a force-model check for one published arrangement. It does not test multi-transducer control, acceleration tracking, levitation stability as an AURA implementation, microgravity, or the long-term project objective.

## Fixed source case

| Item | Published case |
|---|---|
| Body | Expanded-polystyrene sphere, 50 mm diameter, 1.46 g |
| Medium | Air at 25 °C; density 1.18 kg/m³ and sound speed 346 m/s |
| Source | One circular radiating face, 20 mm diameter |
| Frequency | 25,230 Hz |
| Face displacement amplitude | 15 µm |
| Gap, `H` | Distance from transducer radiating face to sphere surface |
| Experimental sweep | 0.1 mm through 30 mm in 0.1 mm steps |
| Primary measured quantity | Axial acoustic radiation force on the sphere, shown in Fig. 5 in gram-force (gf) |
| Measurement arrangement | Sphere on an electronic scale; transducer moved on a motorized linear stage |

The sphere and transducer details are from the article. The numerical method in the source used a rigid sphere and uniform face displacement; those are source-model assumptions, not experimentally established properties.

## Source-figure review

The peer-reviewed author manuscript was reviewed from the Heriot-Watt University repository. Figure 5 overlays a red experimental curve and a black simulation curve. Its axes span `H = 0–30 mm` and force `0–1.8 gf`; the first major measured peak is near `H = 7.2 mm` and about `1.2 gf`. These are visual readings from a plotted figure, not a recovered measurement table.

The figure shows no error bars. The article says the difference between measured and simulated peak values may be attributed to experimental uncertainties and simplifying assumptions, including rigid-sphere and uniform-transducer-motion assumptions. It does not give a pointwise uncertainty, scale accuracy, confidence interval, or numerical uncertainty budget for the measured curve. Its cited supplement is described as covering the estimated maximum sphere size and a suggested extension, rather than the Fig. 5 raw force data. Therefore the plotted line cannot support an invented percentage tolerance or a formal statistical pass/fail result.

The article cites Andrade et al. (2014) for an electronic-balance measurement method. That source's abstract describes a different 20.3 kHz single-axis device with a concave transducer and reflector; it does not establish the accuracy of the 2016 scale or force curve. [DOI: 10.1063/1.4872356](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4872356).

**Primary source:** M. A. B. Andrade, A. L. Bernassau, and J. C. Adamowski, “Acoustic levitation of a large solid sphere,” *Applied Physics Letters* 109, 044101 (2016), [DOI: 10.1063/1.4959862](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4959862). [Heriot-Watt repository record and peer-reviewed manuscript](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/acoustic-levitation-of-a-large-solid-sphere/).

## Primary comparison and uncertainty handling

The primary observable is the entire axial force-versus-gap curve. A future comparison record must preserve the digitized experimental curve as figure-derived data, separately from the paper's simulated curve.

An initial automated trace extraction has been archived at 180 dpi and 360 dpi in [`data/derived/`](../../data/derived/README.md). Both passes use the same author, threshold and axis calibration, so this is a rendering-resolution sensitivity check, **not** an independent second digitization. They return a first measured peak near 1.19 gf at 7.1 mm; their pointwise difference has median 0.00324 gf and maximum 0.06960 gf near the steep first peak. Those values quantify extraction sensitivity only. They are neither experimental uncertainty nor a tolerance.

Before any comparison run:

1. A second independent digitization of the experimental red curve must be made using a separately implemented method or independent operator. Each record must include the image checksum, plot-axis calibration points, point coordinates, and method/version used.
2. The spread between digitizations and the plotted line's pixel thickness will be reported as **figure-reading uncertainty only**. It must not be described as experimental measurement uncertainty.
3. The source's 0.1 mm stage increment will be recorded as sweep spacing, not as the gap measurement accuracy.
4. The comparison report will show the model and digitized measurement across the full gap range, plus pointwise differences and peak location/height differences. No data smoothing, omitted gaps, or re-fitting may be introduced without a recorded reason and preserved original values.
5. Numerical error and sensitivity to model assumptions must be reported separately from figure-reading and source measurement uncertainty.

### Outcome rule

- **PASS:** unavailable for a formal validation claim unless an authoritative source provides or independently measures the experimental uncertainty and a defensible acceptance range is approved before the model run. No arbitrary percentage is permitted.
- **FAIL / not consistent:** may be reported only if a discrepancy is robust to the documented digitization and numerical uncertainties and is not explained by an identified mismatch in the declared case. It is a benchmark disagreement, not proof that the overall AURA hypothesis is false.
- **INDETERMINATE:** mandatory for formal validation if experimental measurement uncertainty remains unavailable, even if the calculated curve visually overlaps the figure. A descriptive statement such as “consistent with the published curve at figure resolution” may accompany it, but must not be called validation or a pass.
- **STOP:** stop comparison if source conditions cannot be matched, the gap convention differs, the measured curve cannot be digitized reproducibly, or numerical results fail their own predeclared convergence checks.

Before computation, assign an immutable experiment ID and record:

- this specification's revision and source DOI;
- body and medium values above, their provenance, and units;
- source-face geometry, frequency, displacement-amplitude convention and gap convention;
- governing equations, model assumptions, boundary conditions, solver/version, numerical error plan and resource estimate;
- digitized data files, axis calibration, image checksum and digitization method;
- primary comparison outputs, the outcome rule above, and any owner-approved change;
- code revision, environment, configuration checksum and generated-output checksums when a run exists.

No run ID or experiment ID is assigned by this design document. No solver or scientific result is approved here.
